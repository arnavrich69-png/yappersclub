# इमली क्यों? · why the imli

A 21 s reel that makes the brand's one obscure reference land for anyone, in the brand's own world.
Built for the profile's pinned row (and a story highlight), so every post after it can lean on it.

## The problem it solves

Dhwanikul is "wrapped like the imli that, Gwalior says, sweetened Tansen's voice". Almost nobody
knows that legend, so the candy, the wrapper and "TANSEN'S RECIPE" read as random. A reference only
works if the audience has it or can get it in seconds. This film gives it to them once, and turns
it from a puzzle into the brand's best story.

## The legend, as Gwalior tells it (checked)

- Tansen, the most famous singer in Indian history and one of the "nine jewels" of Akbar's court, is
  buried in Gwalior, beside his teacher Muhammad Ghaus.
- A tamarind (imli) tree grows beside his grave.
- Gwalior believes that chewing one of its leaves makes your voice sweet; singers still visit the
  tree, and the Tansen music festival is held there every winter.

The film says "GWALIOR SAYS", because it is a belief, not a fact.
Sources: [The Quint on the Tansen Sangeet Samaroh](https://www.thequint.com/lifestyle/travel/tansen-sangeet-samaroh-mp),
[Kupi on the tomb of Tansen](https://kupi.com/en/explore/india/gwalior/tomb-of-tansen),
[Sahapedia on the mausoleums of Gwalior's poets and musicians](https://sahapediaexperiences.org/blog/dastaan-e-maqbara-mausoleums-poets-and-musicians).

The hero words spell the brand out: the question (इमली क्यों?), the man (तानसेन), the promise
(मीठी आवाज़), the gift (इमली), the family (कुल), and finally the name that holds both: ध्वनि KUL.

## How it is made

- The same world as the open call film: one thread, never cut, on the grid line; LANTERN for the
  question, LABEL for the story; Modak heroes, Big Shoulders lines; halftone and grain.
- New: Tansen's tomb, drawn as a candy wrapper illustration (a small pavilion, three arches, a jali,
  corner kiosks, an onion dome), printed in and out through halftone dots.
- Reused: the thread lifted into Tansen's tree (shared with the open call), the wrapper that twists
  shut and is tied, the wrist and its kalava, the lockup's label and wordmark.
- The music is the open call's own language on the three brand sounds: the rising question, the tree
  a note a beat, the two bar tune for the sweet voice, the glide as the thread tightens, the knot.

## How to use it

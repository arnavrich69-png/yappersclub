# इमली क्यों? · why the imli, and why the thread

A 40 s reel that makes the brand's two obscure references land for anyone, in the brand's own world:
why our singers get an imli, and why we tie a red thread on them. Built for the profile's pinned row
(and a story highlight), so every post after it can lean on it.

## The problem it solves

Dhwanikul is "wrapped like the imli that, Gwalior says, sweetened Tansen's voice, and tied with the
red thread that makes you family". Almost nobody knows either story, so the candy, the wrapper,
"TANSEN'S RECIPE" and the kalava read as random. A reference only works if the audience has it or can
get it in seconds. This film gives them both, once, and turns them from puzzles into the brand's best
story.

## The stories, as they are told (checked)

- Tansen, the most famous singer in Indian history, was one of the "nine jewels" of Akbar's court.
  Legend says his Raag Deepak lit lamps and his Raag Megh Malhar brought the rain.
- He is buried in Gwalior, in the tomb complex of his Sufi master Muhammad Ghaus. A tamarind (imli)
  tree grows beside his grave, and Gwalior believes that chewing one of its leaves makes your voice
  sweet. Singers still visit it, and the Tansen Samaroh is held there every December.
- In Indian classical music a guru ties a sacred thread (the ganda) on a student's wrist: the ganda
  bandhan. It joins the student to the guru and the lineage, a musical family.

The film says THEY SAY and GWALIOR SAYS, because these are legends and beliefs, not facts.

The hero words tell both stories by themselves: the question, the man, his lamps, his rain, his

that holds both.

## How it is made

- The same world as the open call film: one thread, never cut, on the grid line; LANTERN for the
  legend, LABEL for the story, PULP for the one peak; Modak heroes, Big Shoulders lines; halftone
  and grain. Above the thread for bars 10 to 12, where the wrists fill the frame below it.
- New: clay diyas hung on the thread by brass hooks (unlit silhouettes, then imli clay, a haldi
  flame and a pool of printed dots; never a glow); rain as printed cream streaks, the scored drops
  splashing on the string; Tansen's tomb as a candy wrapper illustration; the packet's DIRECTIONS
  FOR USE panel; the dictionary brackets under the label.
- Reused: the packet hand (from above this time, lifting the thread), the thread lifted into
  Tansen's tree, the wrapper that twists shut and is tied, the wrists and their kalava, PULP, the
  lockup's label and wordmark.
- The music is the open call's own language on the three brand sounds: the rising question (twice),
  one Sa in a silence, a note a lamp, thunder and rain on the wrapper with drops on the string, the
  tree a note a beat, the two bar tune for the sweet voice and again at the peak, the glide as the
  kalava tightens, the stamp, the final Sa on the knot.

## 1. The idea

**बस तू बाकी है: everything is ready, only you are missing.**

The film reads Tansen's recipe off a Dhwanikul packet. Every ingredient is already in it: the imli
from Tansen's tree, the kalava thread, a song, a stage, a seal. The last line of the ingredients is
blank. A cream tag with no name swings on the thread at the start, in the silence in the middle and
at the end, waiting for one.

Why it works as an open call:
- It tells a performer exactly what they get: an imli, a thread on the wrist, a stage, their name on
  a tag, a place in the kul.
- Gwalior's own legend is the hook, so it reads as ours, not as a template.
- The call to action is an object, the blank tag, not just a line of text.
- The blank tag is the same SUNG BY tag that will carry each performer's name in their clip after
  Night 01 (Brief 03). The open call sets up every post that follows it.
- By the last frame the film has assembled a full Night 01 label, so the end frame doubles as the
  grid cover, with the thread at y = 672.

## 2. One string · एक तार

From the brand's first rough: the thread is one string. In this film it is the only character
and the only instrument. Everything you hear is played on that thread, plus the wrapper (crinkle)
and the stamp (thud). Every note you hear, you see: the thread is plucked, rings, drones,
tightens. No instruments, mics or sound waves on screen. The thread is the music.

What the thread becomes, in order, never cut, never doubled, never faded:

1. A string in the dark that a packet hand plucks: the first note, पहला सुर.
2. One continuous line that draws Tansen's tamarind tree, a branch per note.
3. A line that follows a falling pod down, wraps it and ties both twisted ends: the tied imli.
4. The underline under each ingredient as it is written.
5. A slack line in the silence, the blank tag hanging from it.
6. A string snapped taut that yanks the whole label away like a tablecloth.
7. The string that plays तेरी बारी's tune, ringing as a standing wave.
8. A kalava wound twice round a wrist, its pitch rising as it tightens, tied off as a rakhi.
9. The same thread running on from wrist to wrist: the kul.
10. The grid line at y = 672, strung behind the final headline and tied off with the knot.

## 4. Typography (shaping tested, see `type-test.png`)

- **Big Shoulders** 800 and 900, all caps, tracked 0.12 to 0.2: every English line, 7 words at most,

- **Khand** 600 and 700: small Hindi inside panels and bands (खट्टा मीठा, मीठी डोर).
- **ध्वनि KUL** only ever from the designer's lockup outlines, never typed.
- One dominant word per moment. Nothing critical above y 220, below y 1620 or right of x 940.
- तानसेन की इमली is 946 px wide at 150 px, so it sets at 130 px.

## 5. Colour and texture

The fixed palette only: imli orange is home, haldi is light and bands, cream is letters and panels,
ink is outlines and small text, tamarind is shadows and the PULP world, red is the thread.
No gradients anywhere: light falls off in halftone dots that shrink.
Halftone (14%, 8 px, 18 degrees) and paper grain on every ground. LANTERN gets pale specks on ink.
PULP gets tamarind fibres: darker strands under the grain, and everything in it moves slower.

## 6. Motion

Materials move differently (brand rules, and the guide's "paper is not ink is not thread"):

- **Thread**: elastic; overshoots and settles; rings like a string; never motion blurred.
- **Wrapper**: crinkles, snaps, a slight overshoot as it flattens.
- **Stamp and seal**: fast drop, squash, small rebound, a little ink spread.
- **Modak**: a soft candy bounce, fill a frame before the outline.
- **Label (paper)**: slides with weight and friction, settles flat.
- **Packet hands**: stiff and a little stepped, like the pointing hand on old packets.

Avoid: linear slides, scale from zero, fades, everything centred, everything moving on every beat,
random glitch.

## 7. Camera

One camera, no cuts: the whole film is one journey along one thread. 2.5D, three depth layers
(ground, objects, thread) with a little parallax.

1. It follows the thread through the blank tag's punched hole and comes out in the wrapper world.
2. It pulls back slowly while the tree is drawn.
3. It drops with the falling pod.
4. It pulls back to reveal the packet, then slides down the label to the ingredients.
5. It holds dead still for the silence: the only time it does not move.
6. It is jolted by the yank.
7. It pushes into the light.
8. It pulls back along the thread from wrist to wrist.
9. It settles on the finished label, thread at y = 672.

## 9. Music: एक तार, built in code from the three brand sounds

- **Tempo**: 90 BPM, 20 frames a beat, 80 a bar. 15 bars, 40 seconds.
- **Groove**: keherwa, the eight beat folk groove (dha ge na ti · na ka dhi na), played on found
  sounds. The thud is dha and ge, the crinkle is na and ti, a muted pluck is ka.
- **Drone**: the pluck retuned to a tanpura (Pa, Sa, Sa, low Sa), as in the 10 second test.
- **Melody**: the pluck pitched to Raag Bhupali (Sa Re Ga Pa Dha). It is an evening raga with no sour
  notes, so it reads as sweet. There is a two bar tune: it is asked as a question in bar 1, answered
  in full at तेरी बारी, and resolves to Sa on the final knot.
- **Silence**: one real silence (bar 8, beats 1 and 2). Nothing plays, so the yank hits.
- **Swap-ready**: when you record the real pluck, crinkle and thud, the whole score re-renders with
  them. If you would rather use a track (a member's original or a licensed beat), send it. I will
  beat-map it and the screenplay keeps its shape.

## 12. Self review: what I threw out

- A FLAVOUR seal in the corner plus a PACKED AT seal at the end: that is two seals on one post. The
  flavour now rides a haldi band; PACKED AT is the one seal.
- The ingredients as a list on a static card (template). Each line is written by the thread, and
  each ingredient acts itself out.
- A spotlight that is on from the start (the 10 second test). Light is now the payoff, and the yank
  causes it.
- A hand with a mic or a guitar for "sing": never, by the brand rules. The thread rings instead.
- SINGERS WANTED as the hook: generic. The hook is a Hindi question you hear played on the string:
  पहला सुर किसका?
- Hearts for "kul": generic. The kul is one thread running through many wrists.
- Risk: 40 seconds of reading. Every English line is 7 words or fewer and holds at least 1.5 s, and
  the story still works muted.
